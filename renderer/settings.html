Keywords

Words that make a dictation do something instead of becoming text.

Keywords

Say the keyword first and the rest becomes the query — “Google, what is the capital of Indiana”. %s marks where the query goes; with no %s a URL just gets it appended. Commands run without a shell, so the query stays one argument no matter what is in it. A keyboard macro ignores the query and works the keyboard instead.
How a command runs

The target is a program and its arguments, split on spaces; "quotes" keep words together. %s becomes what you said, as one argument, however many words it is.

It runs directly, not through a shell, and without a window. Nothing it prints is shown, so a command is for starting things, not for reading their output. Pipes, > and variables do not work; point the target at cmd /c or sh -c yourself if you need them.

Give the program's full path. The app does not see your terminal's PATH, and on Windows a .cmd or .bat shim cannot start without a shell. Example:

How a macro is recorded

Click the target field and press the combination. It starts recording as soon as the field has focus, and the combination appears there as you press it. Esc throws the recording away and keeps what was there before; clicking the field again records over it.

The + beside the delete grows it into a macro — a numbered list of steps run in order, with the press you already recorded as the first of them. The + does not add anything by itself: it opens the two kinds of step underneath, and whichever you pick is inserted after it. Every step then carries a + of its own, so press, wait, press is built in the order it runs. Delete your way back down to one press and it goes back to being a single field.

A wait is for the cases where the next key has to land after a menu has opened or a window has come up. Two presses with nothing between them still get a few milliseconds, because keys sent in the same instant can be missed. The longest single wait is 10 seconds and a macro can have 32 steps; while one runs, the app is busy with it and you cannot dictate.

The keys go to whatever window has the caret — the same window a transcript would have been typed into. The rest of what you said is ignored: a macro has nothing to do with a query. A step that cannot be read stops the whole macro rather than part of it, and the overlay says so.
